Jack Grealish: What next for England winger after 'breach of club discipline'?

Grealish dropped over 'breach of club discipline' - what now?

David Moyes says it was the club's decision to leave Jack Grealish out of Everton's Carabao Cup win over Newcastle United because of "a breach of club discipline".

Everton did not disclose any further details, but the decision is understood to have arisen from an unauthorised absence from training.

"You probably know I'm not going to give any details," said Moyes after Everton's three-one win at Hill Dickinson Stadium set up a quarter-final tie at League Two Fleetwood Town.

"It's a club decision, it's something I don't want to speak too much about."

Moyes made it clear he supported the decision to leave out England winger Grealish, thirty-one, who is on loan from Premier League rivals Manchester City.

Grealish spent a large chunk of last season with the Toffees under a similar arrangement, scoring twice and providing six assists in twenty appearances, before sustaining a season-ending foot injury in January.

He returned to City and came on as a substitute during their three-nil Community Shield defeat by Arsenal at Wembley in August.

The former Aston Villa player, who cost City one hundred million pounds in twenty twenty-one, also featured as a substitute during their two-one win over Bournemouth on the opening weekend of the Premier League season.

He rejoined Everton before the transfer window closed and has featured five times for them this season in the Premier League.

Grealish also played in Everton's EFL Cup third-round win over Wolves.

Asked if Grealish would return for the Premier League game at Newcastle on Monday, Moyes added: "I wouldn't give any team news away."

What next for Grealish?

Grealish is in the final year of his reported three hundred thousand pounds a week contract at Manchester City.

He was discarded by Pep Guardiola in the Spaniard's final season in charge, being allowed to join Everton on a season-long loan deal.

There were moments where he looked to be getting back to his best while playing for the Toffees before his foot injury.

Such was his form at the beginning of last season, he claimed the Premier League's player-of-the-month award for August and quickly became a fans' favourite.

"He is a player who has fallen in love with the right shade of blue, but ultimately he is a City player and we have to respect that," Everton chief executive Angus Kinnear told BBC Radio Merseyside in August.

"What I do know is that the supporters and the team embraced him and treated him as one of our own. That gave us a good platform to have a longer term conversation with him.

"The most important thing is that he is back fit after he had a serious injury, so I was delighted to see him get some minutes in the Community Shield."

Grealish impressed to such an extent last season that Moyes openly spoke about how much the player was "loved" at the club.

The mood, however, appears to have changed.

At a time when the Everton boss is relying on all his senior players to step up after a disappointing summer transfer window left his squad thin, there are now questions about whether Grealish's breach of club discipline will be the beginning of the end of his career at Everton.

Grealish missed last Saturday's two-nil defeat at Arsenal with illness and, according to reports, missed training on Monday without permission.

He returned to training at Everton's Finch Farm complex but is expected to be fined for his unauthorised absence.

Grealish has thirty-nine England caps and has scored four goals for his country.

He has not played for the Three Lions since scoring in a three-one Nations League win against Finland in Helsinki in twenty twenty-four.

Everton, who have not won a major trophy since nineteen ninety-five, did not need him as they brushed past Newcastle to reach the last eight of the EFL Cup.

While Moyes refused to go into details about the breach of discipline, he did praise the players on duty on Thursday for not allowing Grealish's absence to distract them in what was an important cup tie.

"The one thing about Everton is that it has got good values behind it," added Moyes.

"All the players stand up to the values on pitch and off the pitch.

"The players did a brilliant job tonight.

"We are limited in some areas but we are doing our best to keep challenging."
