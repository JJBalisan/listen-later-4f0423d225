Eliud Kipchoge, 41, breaks course record at Melbourne Marathon

Kipchoge, forty-one, breaks course record at Melbourne Marathon. A report from the BBC.

Two-time Olympic champion Eliud Kipchoge says "his body is still peaking" after winning the Melbourne Marathon in a course record time at the age of forty-one.

The Kenyan crossed the line in two hours, nine minutes, ten seconds for his seventeenth career win over the distance and first since the Berlin Marathon in twenty twenty-three.

"I believe that my body is still peaking, still able to compete with the next generation," Kipchoge said.

"The oldest athlete is still competing but I am happy to run with the Gen Zs."

The course was partly raced along the Australian Formula One track at Albert Park and finished inside the iconic Melbourne Cricket Ground, the M C G.

"On the course, it was really wonderful, I enjoyed seeing people standing, cheering, and shouting my name," added Kipchoge, who slowed down inside the M C G to wave to the fans.

"It was actually different, running on a beautiful course, but also running on the Formula One track, and above all else, finishing on a cricket ground.

"It makes me proud to run with the younger generation and inspire the running community in Melbourne."

The rest of the field

Australian Thomas do Canto was runner-up in two hours, ten minutes, thirty-five seconds, with compatriot Reece Edwards third.

New Zealand's Camille French won the women's race in two hours, twenty-eight minutes, twenty-five seconds, followed by Australian Sinead Diver and Nichola Sheridan of Ireland.

The world tour

The Melbourne Marathon was the third leg on Kipchoge's world tour, on which he hopes to complete a marathon on all seven continents, including Antarctica, over two years.

Each marathon will raise funds for the Eliud Kipchoge Foundation, which focuses on education, environmental sustainability and health.

Career background

Kipchoge is considered one of the greatest long-distance runners of all time, winning two Olympic marathon golds, plus a silver and bronze over five thousand metres.

His honours also include a world title, twelve World Marathon Major victories and two official world records.

In twenty nineteen he became the first person to break the two-hour marathon barrier under special conditions in Vienna, clocking an unofficial time of one hour, fifty-nine minutes, forty seconds.
