Is AI Writing American Law? — Effort

Is AI Writing American Law? An Effort investigation.

We scanned every bill in the US Congress since twenty twenty-three to find out. AI models are writing our laws. An Effort investigation found the fraction of bills in the US Congress written by AI has tripled in the past three years.

Among congressional bills with preambles and findings sections, AI generated text rose from just under two percent to six point four percent likely AI written, from the first quarter of twenty twenty-three to the last quarter, the second quarter of twenty twenty-six. The rate among bills with no preambles and findings is significantly lower. A large number of these bills are renewals, extensions and other copies of pre-AI legislation.

The Epstein Files Act

The Epstein Files Transparency Act is the only majority AI-written bill in our sample that became law. It is one of the most widely covered and politically divisive bills in the past year.

We confirmed that it was majority-written by AI using the state of the art AI detection model, Pangram four. "Pangram's false positive rate is below one in ten thousand: it is highly likely that text flagged as AI-generated by Pangram was at least significantly if not fully generated by AI," Pangram's cofounder and CEO Max Spero told Effort.

The Epstein Files Transparency Act may violate the House AI use policy, according to a POPVOX summary. Neither the House or Senate AI policy is public, but the POPVOX foundation summarised the House policy.

There is no public disclosure of AI use on any bill. The Epstein Transparency Act would be a clear case of "finalizing legislation" with AI, which the policy prohibits. Staff are likely unaware of the House AI policy. As of publication, there have been no public consequences for undisclosed AI use in congress.

Remarks, parties and topics

AI text is even more prevalent in Extensions of Remarks in the Congressional Record. AI wrote about fifteen percent of them last quarter, more than twice the rate for bills. We found no statistically significant difference between Democratic and Republican use of AI.

Across all policy areas we investigated, law enforcement and finance bills are the likeliest to be written by AI. Other factors have small, but statistically significant effects on likely AI usage.

Institutional mechanisms

Topic coefficients point to institutional mechanisms. Financial bills flagged as AI are sponsored by committees where a majority of members come from corporate backgrounds. They tend to adopt AI faster than members from traditional government careers. At the other end, Labor bills are usually drafted with large unions, which are slower adopters, and are reviewed more carefully given their implications for the voter base.

Crime bills stood out because the mechanism was less obvious. One hypothesis is that many are responses to recent events requiring fast turnarounds. For example, in response to the Los Angeles ICE raids on the sixth of June, twenty twenty-five, a bill was introduced in the House on the tenth of June, twenty twenty-five, demanding condemnation of acts of violence against law enforcement officers. Similarly, in response to the assassination of Charlie Kirk on the tenth of September, twenty twenty-five, a bill was introduced in the Senate on the fifth of November, twenty twenty-five, demanding the death penalty for ideologically motivated crime.

Looking ahead

As AI becomes an ordinary part of an increasing number of Americans' lives, it is likely to become a more important part of the lawmaking process. The ability to detect AI use will shape norms around using and disclosing AI. "Pangram is a useful tool for enforcing AI policies, especially in high stakes situations such as legislation," Spero told Effort.

Data and method notes

We use editlens Llama three point two, three B, an open-source version of Pangram, calibrated via Pangram three point three point two to improve accuracy. Bills from the one hundred eighteenth and one hundred nineteenth Congress are analysed. Daily records from the same two Congresses are analysed for the Extensions of Remarks.

To understand the institutional mechanisms that drive AI use, we built a linear probability model to predict whether a bill is flagged AI or not. The accompanying graph shows ninety percent confidence intervals.

Tables

The first table covers the two thousand nine hundred ninety-four bills of the one hundred nineteenth Congress, of which one hundred seventeen, or three point nine percent, were flagged as AI. House bills were flagged at four point nine percent against two point one percent for the Senate. Solo-sponsored bills were flagged at six percent against three point five percent for bills with cosponsors. Bills from sponsors aged sixty-three or younger were flagged at five point three percent against two point five percent for older sponsors. Shorter bills were flagged more often than longer ones, at four point nine percent against two point nine percent. Crime bills were flagged at eleven point four percent and finance bills at eleven point one percent, against about three and a half percent for other topics. None of the sixty-three labor bills was flagged.

A second table breaks down committees. The majority of the AI bills come from the Banking and Financial Services committee, where forty-nine of seventy-seven bills, or sixty-four percent, are listed, with smaller counts for committees such as Intelligence, Foreign Affairs, Natural Resources, Transportation and Infrastructure, and Science, Space and Technology.

A third table ranks topics by the share of bills flagged as AI. Crime and law enforcement is highest at eleven point four percent, then finance at eleven point one percent, government operations and policy at eight point two percent, transportation and public works at seven point five percent, and immigration at six point six percent. Lower down are education at four point nine percent, armed forces and national security at three point nine percent, and health at three point six percent. Bills with no topic flagged sit at two point two percent, and international affairs at two percent. Environmental protection and labor and employment are both at zero percent. Overall, one hundred seventeen of two thousand nine hundred ninety-four bills, or three point nine percent, were flagged.
