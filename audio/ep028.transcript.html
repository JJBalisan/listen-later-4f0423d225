Effective Altruism is Buying Political Staff

Effective Altruism is Buying Political Staff, from Effort. Subtitle: Coefficient Giving's documents show they are "placing" hand-picked staff into government offices.

AI Safety megadonors Dustin Moskovitz and Sam Bankman-Fried have spent hundreds of millions of dollars on political campaigns and a further half a billion on think tanks. What has that gotten them?

The placement strategy

An archived Open Philanthropy grant table obtained by Effort shows a concerted "placement" strategy by Open Philanthropy to put their hand-picked employees in US and UK government offices.

While Open Philanthropy, now rebranded to Coefficient Giving, no longer announces its "placements", multiple sources tell me they continue to employ this strategy within the Trump administration.

Bankman-Fried was the third largest Democratic donor in twenty twenty-two. Dustin Moskovitz was their third largest donor in twenty twenty, then the second largest in twenty twenty-four. At the same time, Moskovitz and Bankman-Fried funded a half-billion dollar apparatus of policy advocacy and media.

Their donations not only resulted in policies, but embedded personnel in the Biden and Trump administrations, Congress, and the UK government. The UK's AI Safety Institute, or AISI, has several senior employees previously employed by or funded by Moskovitz and Bankman-Fried.

The UK AI Safety Institute

A table titled "The UK AI Safety Institute is Managed by Effective Altruists" lists seven people in AISI roles, with their past affiliations and the money tied to those affiliations from Moskovitz and Bankman-Fried.

Benjamin Hilton, Head of Alignment, came from 80,000 Hours, which received thirty-nine point three six million dollars in awards from Moskovitz.

Jade Leung, Chief Technology Officer, came from GovAI, which received sixteen point seven nine million dollars in awards from Moskovitz and four hundred one thousand, five hundred thirty-seven dollars from Bankman-Fried's Long-Term Future Fund.

Joseph Bloom, Model Transparency lead, received a fifty thousand dollar personal grant from the Long-Term Future Fund.

David Krueger, Taskforce Research Director from September to December twenty twenty-three, had a personal grant from Open Philanthropy, one point two five million dollars in research awards from Moskovitz, and two hundred thousand dollars from the Long-Term Future Fund.

Paul Christiano, a Taskforce external adviser appointed in September twenty twenty-three, was an Open Philanthropy employee.

Alex Holness-Tofts, who works on strategy and operations for the Alignment Project, came from the Centre for Effective Altruism, which received one hundred seven point eight million dollars in awards from Moskovitz and about fourteen million dollars from an FTX award.

Tom Davidson, a former research scientist, was also an Open Philanthropy employee.

Congress and the Horizon Fellowship

President Joe Biden attempted to create an AI Safety Institute through executive order. This institute was later severely limited by Trump and renamed the Center for AI Standards and Innovation, or CAISI.

At the same time, Effective Altruists created a program to pay the salaries of EA Congressional staffers and place them with US Representatives and Senators, called the Horizon Fellowship. These staffers work directly with Representatives and Senators and significantly impact the way they vote and the bills they author.

Staffers, even those whose salaries are subsidized by other organizations, are supposed to represent the values of the members of Congress they work for. According to several senior staffers on background, that was not the case with Horizon fellows. "He went to Democrat policy meetings, and tried to hand Democrat policies to the Senator he worked for," one senior staffer in a Republican Senator's office told me. EA fellowships have placed over fifty staffers in Congress and the administrative branch.

Horizon has received over twelve million dollars from Open Philanthropy, now Coefficient Giving. Flush with EA money, Horizon doubled their salaries. Horizon called for "AI rapid-response" hires. Adopting the doomer view could triple or quadruple a policy staffer's salary.

Sophia Brown and CAISI

The former Strategy and Partnerships Director of the Horizon Fellowship, Sophia Brown, was hired into the Trump administration's CAISI, their successor to the AI Safety Institute. Brown was hired as an Intergovernmental Personnel Act hire through the Moskovitz-funded Foundation for American Innovation, or FAI, one former FAI employee told me. Brown holds a "Senior Advisor" role at FAI and was formerly their director of AI Policy. Despite its name, FAI's AI policies restrict American AI innovation.
