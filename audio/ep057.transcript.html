No, I don’t want ‘normal milk’

No, I don’t want ‘normal milk’. The author is not named.

If you’re a vegan, you’ve probably been in a situation similar to this.

You walk into a cafe looking to get a coffee or a cup of tea. You quickly scan the menu on the board behind the counter to see if the cafe lists any plant milk options. Upon being unable to see any listing for a plant milk, you decide to ask the barista.

‘Sorry,’ they reply, ‘we only serve normal milk.’

You thank them for letting you know and then make your way out of the cafe, albeit with a bitter taste in your mouth and not the taste of the oat milk flat white that you had been hoping for.

It’s probably unlikely that the phrase ‘normal milk’ was used in a malicious or ill-intentioned way. That being said, I do still distinctly remember the reaction I received in a coffee shop back in twenty sixteen when I asked if they had any vegan options. Flash forward ten years and the coffee shop does now have vegan options – although not nearly enough to remove the grudge I’ve personally held on to for over a decade.

Why the word matters

The use of the word ‘normal’ to describe cow’s milk can be seen as a simple reflection of the fact that cow’s milk is still the most widely consumed milk. However, while true, this overlooks how the use of the word ‘normal’ has far more significance and meaning behind it than that alone.

After all, the reason why the phrase ‘normal milk’ is frustrating for us as vegans is not because it reminds us that most people consume it – by the virtue of us being in an environment where we are asking for plant milk, we are already more than fully aware that the majority of people consume dairy products.

It also can’t be argued that the reason people use the phrase ‘normal milk’ is because it’s easier or faster to say. If, for example, we didn’t have the phrase ‘dairy milk’ or ‘cow’s milk’ and the only two choices were ‘normal milk’ and ‘mammalian bovine lactation milk’, then simplifying it down to the two-syllable option would be more understandable.

If we take the situation I described at the beginning, it’s also not because the cafe didn’t have a plant milk option available. If the barista had said they only served ‘dairy milk’, that would leave less of a bitter taste. Inversely, if the cafe did have oat milk available but the barista asked if we wanted oat milk or normal milk, that would still leave a bitter taste.

So what is it about the word ‘normal’?

Normality and the bandwagon effect

For so long the word ‘milk’ has simply meant dairy milk or cow’s milk. This isn’t so strictly the case any more, especially as there are also non-vegans who opt to consume plant milks. However, alongside the increase in plant milk consumption, there has also been an increase in awareness about why people should opt for plant milks over cow’s milk. While more needs to be done to shine a light on the harms of dairy farming, it is also true that there is more understanding now about the environmental and ethical issues that come from exploiting cows for their secretions.

This could provide an additional layer of explanation as to why we often hear dairy being referred to as ‘normal milk’; after all, consuming cow’s milk can’t really be a concern because it’s so ‘normal’. This also plays into the bandwagon effect, a cognitive bias that soothes us into believing that our choices are acceptable because they’re the same choices that the majority of people make.

If a barista spends all day calling cow’s milk ‘normal milk’ then that is reaffirming to them over and over again that their choices are normal. Likewise, if a customer is asked what milk they want they are being presented with choices which mean that they don’t have to choose cow’s milk. This might cause some form of cognitive dissonance where they realise that consuming cow’s milk is a choice. Instead of requesting dairy milk or cow’s milk, they might ask for ‘normal milk’, as doing so acts as a justification for why they are choosing that milk over the other milk options available to them.

The power of subtle language

Language choices can be very subtle, but they are also very powerful. It’s also not necessarily the case that people consciously realise they are making the language choices they are, even though those choices might be subtly distancing them from reflecting more deeply and deliberately on their consumption of animal products.

One example is when we as vegans hear people say, ‘I could never go vegan’. Of course, the person almost certainly could, but they don’t want to. Using the phrasing ‘could never’ reinforces to the person that they don’t need to meaningfully engage with veganism as they ‘could never go vegan’ anyway.

Normal and abnormal as value judgements

One of the implications that arises when cow’s milk is called ‘normal milk’ is that plant-based milks are ‘abnormal’. Instead of simply meaning a deviation from what is typical, the word ‘abnormal’ implies that something is strange, unwanted or even worrying. Interestingly, if a cafe has cow’s milk and oat milk, a barista wouldn’t ask if you wanted ‘normal’ or ‘abnormal milk’. This suggests that the word ‘abnormal’ is viewed as being a pejorative and not a neutral statement or simply a reflection on purchasing trends.

However, if ‘abnormal milk’ is understood as carrying with it a value judgement, it’s also true that referring to cow’s milk as ‘normal milk’ also carries with it a value judgement – in this case, a positive one. This is one of the main reasons we as vegans wouldn’t call cow’s milk ‘normal milk’ and is why some non-vegans do call cow’s milk ‘normal milk’, as the use of the word ‘normal’ brings with it a value judgement.

Admittedly, I think that one reason why we as vegans can find the phrase ‘normal milk’ frustrating is because it also implies something about us. It implies that we are abnormal and strange, that our choices are inherently othering and push us towards the edges of usual societal participation. We’re not ordering a coffee like the majority of other people, we are ordering a coffee that separates us from the ‘normal’ people around us.

While this again might be true when viewed simply through the lens of purchasing a product that fewer people statistically purchase, similarly to what I just mentioned, the words ‘normal’ and ‘abnormal’ are commonly used as words that describe someone’s character and personality. If someone says, ‘that person is not normal’, it is usually an insult. Likewise, if someone says, ‘I like that person, they just seem very normal’, the word ‘normal’ is being used to say something positive about the character of the person they are describing, hence why they like them.

The ethical concern

There’s also a deeper ethical concern. Calling cow’s milk ‘normal milk’ suggests that what happens to dairy cows should be viewed as acceptable. It shows how deceptive and deeply embedded the animal farming sector is that people would call an industry that forcibly impregnates cows, takes their babies away from them and then slaughters them ‘normal’, while simultaneously implying that choices that don’t perpetuate these cruel practices are ‘abnormal’.

Plus, it’s obvious that people are deliberately kept in the dark about what happens in the dairy industry considering that there isn’t more outrage and discussion about what happens to bulls and cows for the process of artificial insemination to take place. The practice of forced insemination is as creepy as it is morally wrong, and it clearly proves why cow’s milk shouldn’t be viewed as ‘normal’.

Animal farming has created a global and systemic system that has normalised violence and cruelty and has positioned a world with less suffering, pain and death as abnormal. It has created a morally backwards reality that corrupts people into engaging in a system of rampant immorality. It muddies a moral issue that shouldn’t be controversial and leads to a state of ethical hypocrisy where people condemn some forms of immorality towards animals while funding other and often more severe forms.

Conclusion

This further reinforces why the value judgement that comes from the word ‘normal’ is important to recognise. When cow’s milk is called ‘normal milk’, the word ‘normal’ is used as a prescriptive term – rather than simply being an acknowledgement that this is the way things currently are, the word ‘normal’ is used to say that this is the way things should be.

So, no, I don’t want ‘normal milk’, and hopefully one day we’ll realise why cow’s milk shouldn’t be viewed as ‘normal’ at all.
