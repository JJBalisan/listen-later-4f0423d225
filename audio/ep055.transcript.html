I plan to drop everything to push for an AI pause

I plan to drop everything to push for an AI pause.

I might have less than a year left to affect the insane, reckless, Faustian race to A S I, and I plan to spend that time doing everything I can to push for a pause.

I currently think there's a decently high chance that by default, there's six to twelve months left before my personal point of no return: the point after which my own actions no longer plausibly affect the future. This is different from other people's points of no return: for example, people like Dario Amodei, the CEO of Anthropic, or Donald Trump may have slightly longer times to wake up and change course. This is also different from other timelines that people sometimes throw around, like timelines to A G I or A S I or fully automated remote-worker or whatever. However, it is the most important for my own decisions.

I do not defend this position here. Other people have written partial explanations that I like. I may also write more accessible explainers in the near future.

Regardless, humanity is absolutely not ready for what comes next, whether that's human extinction, complete loss of control to rogue AIs or a few oligarchs, or something far weirder.

While I don't have strong confidence that I actually only have six to twelve months left, I think very few people are working on this by default, and AI Safety is growing very quickly, though still far smaller than needed, and newcomers to the field can cover the scenarios where we have more time.

Given this, I think my best strategy is to push hard for a pause on training AI: specifically a complete moratorium on training stronger AI models than the ones we currently have and severe limitations on AI R and D, until scientists, policymakers, and the general public have more time to understand what's going on and catch up.

I think pushing for a pause would be the best use of my time. While it'd be very difficult to execute, it is less robustly doomed than trying to make things better on the margin and slightly increasing the chances that the crazy race to superhuman AI is conducted safely. I'm not very confident in this position and do not defend it in detail here, though I wrote some other strategies I've considered below in the next section.

I'm less opinionated about what happens after a pause. Reasonable options range from resuming carefully under real engineering standards to pausing indefinitely, if humanity collectively decides that the risks posed by superhuman AI is too high.

Regardless, we should not be sleepwalking into disaster. A pause gives humanity time to regroup and rethink things.

Other strategies

What are other strategies I could be pursuing?

Is pausing the best strategy? I think so, but I'm not confident. Here are three options that I've considered and tentatively rejected. I've also considered other options, but they seem even more doomed, at least for me.

First, help key decisionmakers "manage the transition better."

If a pause won't happen, I'd rather the transition to superhuman AI be done well, or okay, or at least poorly, rather than disastrously.

I considered trying to join Anthropic or OpenAI. I currently think I wouldn't be a good fit. I dislike the organizations too much, and working on a doomsday machine to make it slightly less doomed just doesn't sit well with me. I might still re-consider it if nonconflicted people argued me into believing that the impact is high enough, and I should swallow my disgust.

I also considered working to help government people re-orient and make better non-pause decisions. This still seems like a live option to me, and I will continue looking for potentially good options.

Second, macrostrategy automation and trying to solve non-alignment AI issues with AI assistance.

Under some models of AI progress, the most urgent time isn't now but several months in the future, when we'll have three to six months of rapidly accelerated non-technical research due to AI assistance, before my own irrelevance. Maybe I'd have a five to one hundred times speedup on non-alignment problems that matter a lot, like how to counteract the effects of AI propaganda or making sure humanity doesn't go insane through the transition.

Preparing for that time seems somewhat competitive, and there are a relatively small number of people who can do this well, myself marginally included.

I currently feel less excited about this than working towards a pause, but I will continue thinking about it and talking to my colleagues at Forethought about this possibility.

Third, focus on counteracting AI persuasion in short-timelines worlds.

An angle that I feel somewhat excited about and drawn to is trying to help harden specific decision-makers and decision-making against future AI superhuman persuasion, which is my research area this year. While somewhat less likely with faster timelines than slower ones, I still think there are significant worlds where superhuman AI persuasion will be a big part of AI takeover attempts, and hardening within-AI-company and U S government decision processes against AI persuasion seems fairly competitive with other options, and plays well into my research interests and backgrounds.

I can imagine doing this either within or outside of the existing institutions.

For everyone else

Now, for people not currently working on AI safety.

A very important strategic consideration here is that because time to the point of no return might be very short but we can't know for sure, and we likely may never know for sure until it's too late, the safety field should likely bifurcate strategies, where some people work on the assumption of very short, under one year, time to have an impact, and other people work on the assumption of more time to have an impact.

Since the field is growing very quickly but it takes people time to get up to speed, a natural bifurcation is for most people currently in the field to work under the roughly one year clock, and most people currently outside of it to get up to speed and do things that are more promising given more time.

All things considered, I think those worlds are still very salient, either because we're wrong about the future speed of automation of AI research, or because the pause actually worked. However, I don't have advice for how to join the AI safety effort later on, over and beyond the standard advice.

What happens after a pause

Options that seem salient to me right now for what comes after a pause.

A, unpause and proceed ahead on an "AI as normal engineering" assumption, except we take normal engineering safety standards seriously, rather than the slapdash "safety" and "security" measures that currently plague those companies. A note on this: treating AI risks as solvable normal engineering feels doomed to me, but not as completely doomed as treating them as solvable by normal engineering and then not applying normal engineering safety standards, which is the current default.

B, unpause and proceed according to an international verified slowdown between the US and China, with total research transparency and extensive checks on safety between each development cycle.

C, don't unpause until companies have safety cases that they can constrain the risk of further AI development below a manageable threshold, those safety cases are verified by external evaluators and openly communicated and debated, and humanity agrees that unpausing is worth the risk.

D, pause for six months, decide whether the risks of unpausing outweigh the benefits, if not, pause for one year, then do this again, pause for two years, then four, then ten, perhaps, and so forth.

E, do limited AI research into safety, and don't unpause until we discover AI architectures that are provably or demonstrably much safer than the status quo.

F, unpause with a focus on specialized AI architectures for specific tasks, like self-driving cars and medicine, and don't do AI research into R S I or other dangerous scary general AI.

And finally, don't unpause, period, until humanity changes our minds; the technology for supplanting all of human labor and cognition is too dangerous for Man.
